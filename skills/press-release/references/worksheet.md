# Press Release Worksheet

The intake checklist for a press release. Use it to identify what's **missing** after mining
the user's prompt — not as a script to read top to bottom. Items marked **(essential)** are
needed for a credible draft; ask for those if absent. The rest sharpen the release but should
be inferred or omitted rather than interrogated.

## Objectives & positioning

- **Press release objectives** — what is this release meant to accomplish?
- **Role in larger PR goals** — how does this announcement position the company as a market
  leader? What narrative does it advance?
- **Target audience (essential)** — who is this for? IT trade press, vertical/industry press,
  business press, developer press? This shapes tone and which messages lead.

## The announcement

- **What, specifically, are we announcing? (essential)** — the concrete fact: a product launch,
  general availability, a customer win, funding, a partnership, a program, a milestone.
- **The company (essential)** — name plus one line on what it does (for the boilerplate and
  first reference).
- **Ideal headline / subhead** — the user's dream headline, if they have one. Otherwise propose
  one.
- **The one takeaway (essential)** — the single thing you want a reader to understand from this
  announcement. Everything in the release should serve it.

## Messaging

- **3 key messages (essential)** — the core points to land. When eliciting, prompt with: why is
  the news compelling? How does a customer or partner benefit? How is it different from what
  competitors have done or from the past?
- **Support points** — evidence for the messages: specific features, capabilities, numbers,
  benchmarks, proof points.

## Quotes & third parties

- **External quote source (essential)** — ideally a customer; if not, an analyst or industry
  expert. **Never a company employee.** Capture name + title. If none is available, the release
  will mark the external quote as a placeholder to fill later.
- **Company spokesperson (essential)** — name + title of the internal executive who gives the
  company quote.
- **Third-party contacts** — partners, customers, analysts involved in the announcement; their
  name, email, phone where relevant (for coordination, not the release body).
- **Ideal quote messages** — what message should each quote reflect? External quotes should tie
  industry context to the value proposition; the company quote should reiterate function and
  value.

## Logistics

- **Pricing / availability (essential)** — when is it available, through what channel (direct,
  partner/channel, beta, waitlist), and at what price if disclosed.
- **More info** — URL or destination for readers who want details.
