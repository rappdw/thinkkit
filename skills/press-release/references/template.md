# Press Release Template — Six-Section Structure

The output structure every release follows. Six sections, in order. Each section below gives its
purpose and a short worked example. The examples use a generic fictional company ("Northwind")
to model voice and shape — swap in the user's real details.

Lead the body with a **headline** and optional **subhead**, then write the six sections.

---

## 1. Opener — ideally three sentences

The lede. Three beats, ideally three sentences:

- **NEWS** — the fact you're announcing. The launch, the availability, the customer win — the
  plain fact.
  *"Northwind today launched the industry's first combined threat-discovery and
  incident-response platform."*
- **NEW** — what's new and different / what makes it newsworthy. Echoes the headline and first
  sentence but adds a bit more detail in basic language (use synonyms, don't just repeat).
  *"It is the first product to pair automated zero-day detection with one-click network
  isolation, so a discovered threat can be contained the moment it's found."*
- **COOL / IMPLICATIONS** — the value to the outlet's readers; why they should care.
  *"As security teams struggle to keep pace with attacks, the platform lets fewer skilled staff
  handle more threats faster — cutting both risk and response cost by an order of magnitude."*

## 2. Factoid paragraph — a few sentences

Hand the reporter the objective, third-party context that makes the story feel researched.

- **NUMBERS & STATS** — relevant industry figures from credible sources, footnoted. (A short
  search of the right terms usually surfaces what you need.) Mark anything unverified as
  `[VERIFY: source]`.
  *"In the past year, 46% of large enterprises reported at least one breach. [VERIFY: source]
  Yet the average gap between breach and discovery is 43 days, while time-to-hire for skilled
  analysts has risen 22% — a widening mismatch between threats and capacity. [VERIFY: source]"*
- **RESULT** — the punchline / customer-impact statement that segues into the product.
  *"The result is mounting cost and exposure as undetected intrusions linger."*

## 3. External quote

Third-party validation. Most credible voice in the release.

- **SOURCE** — ideally a customer. If necessary, an analyst or industry expert. **Never a
  company employee.** If no source is available yet, insert a clearly marked placeholder.
- **QUOTE** — ties the external context from section 2 to the company's value proposition.
  *"'Attackers move faster than we can hire, so things slip — either undetected or buried in
  alert noise,' said Dana Lee, CISO at Meridian Health. 'An automated detect-and-respond loop
  is exactly what lets a lean team do more, and do it better.'"*

## 4. Product details & facts — a few sentences

What the offering actually is, in plain language.

- **DETAILS** — describe the offering simply, and call out how it differs from alternatives that
  stop short.
  *"Northwind's platform combines detection and response in one workflow. It continuously scans
  for novel threats and — unlike tools that only alert — automatically isolates the affected
  systems from the network."*
- **PUNCHLINE / VALUE** — always close by tying back to concrete customer value.
  *"By containing threats automatically the instant they're found, customers avoid the costly
  window between a risky click and a manual response — saving millions in potential breach
  costs."*

## 5. Company quote

The company's own voice. Reiterate function and value — state the value twice, in different
words.

- **SOURCE** — a company spokesperson (name + title).
- **QUOTE**
  *"'Response time is everything in containing a breach, and we're the first to automate both
  the find and the fix,' said Priya Rao, VP of Product at Northwind. 'Customers who measured
  response in days are now seeing minutes — and that translates directly into fewer breaches and
  real dollars saved.'"*

## 6. Availability & more info

- **AVAILABILITY** — when it's available and how to get it (direct, channel/partner, beta).
  *"Northwind Threat Platform is available now through direct sales and certified channel
  partners."*
- **PRICING** — include if disclosed; omit if not.
- **MORE INFO** — where to learn more.
  *"For details, visit northwind.example/platform."*

---

## Drafting reminders

- Keep the opener to three sentences if at all possible.
- Plain language throughout — assume the reporter is smart but not a domain expert.
- Every claim should trace to a real input. Mark inferences `[ASSUMPTION: ...]`, gaps
  `[NEEDS INPUT: ...]`, and unverified stats `[VERIFY: ...]`.
- Optionally close with a one-paragraph **boilerplate** ("About [Company]") if the user provides
  company background.
